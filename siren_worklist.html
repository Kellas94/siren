Eight lines below his work, a block written for the old editor tab was still painting the new one disabled: it read “Guided”, told you the visual builder could not help, and worked anyway when you clicked it. A control that names an action, performs it, and says it cannot. Rounds 7, 8 and 9 each failed the same pass before it; all four were caught before the live file, by driving the build with real keyboard and mouse rather than reading a diff. The rule everything else is judged against

Set by the owner, 24 August 2026. Not a task — the test every task has to pass.

standing
The rule

No promise the app cannot keep

“Nu vrem sa ajungem AI slop — functii care nu merg si nu sunt testate. Sa nu promitem ce nu putem livra.”

A slogan is unenforceable, so here it is as four things that can be checked. Each one comes from a defect found on 24 August, not from a principle someone liked the sound of.

Every round is measured against these four before it ships
1

A test that asks the right question

Structural validity is not enough. A raster PDF is a perfectly valid PDF; a one-picture slide is a perfectly valid PowerPoint. Both passed every check for months. The test must ask what kind of thing is inside — not “is this a PDF” but “how many characters can I extract from it?” Answer: zero, three times.

Now enforced by qa_exports/run_export_fidelity.js
2

Honest about this thing, not in general

The app is allowed not to support something. It is not allowed to offer it. A builder that presents six numbered steps over a diagram it cannot touch is a promise, and it is broken for 13 of 19 types. Per-type honesty, checked per type — a global claim that is true on average is false to the person in front of it.

Round 7 was two thirds this — and two of its four jobs failed it
3

No control that cannot work

Remove it, or disable it and say why. No dead panels, no chip maintained on every render and never shown, no instruction to “click a part to find its line” where a real mouse can never reach. If a person can see it, it must do something — or tell them why it will not.

Four in 1.67.0, two more in 1.68.0 · both of those found by looking at a picture
4

Measured on the path a person actually takes

The newest rule, and the one that caught the biggest defect. Every PDF claim in the 1.65.0 changelog — compression, italic fidelity, native gradients — was true, and all of it was measured on the deck export. Nobody opened the plain diagram PDF. A claim verified on a neighbouring path is not verified.

Applies to the changelog as much as to the code

Round 11 — the tour that eats what you type

About 2.3 seconds into a first visit, the welcome tour takes the keyboard. Typing “Review request” into the box the product points you at leaves “Revie” — nine characters destroyed, focus on the tour’s Next button, and the tour advanced to 3/6 by the person’s own keystrokes. Nothing warns; the status strip still reads “Syntax looks valid.”

Then: a 44-block diagram exports to PowerPoint as 6pt text in 4.7pt boxes, clipped and silent; and following a reference from a document opens the diagram but selects nothing, because a four-line branch throws away the block it had already resolved.

It is first-run only — press F5 to check it and you measure a path where the tour never appears
Done

The second editor slot says what it is

Measured across all twenty diagram types: the builder can edit three, sequence has an editor of its own, and sixteen opened a panel that announced “Build without code”, then explained the type is code-first, and left nine disabled controls on screen.

The slot now reads Build, Sequence or Guided, and pressing it goes where the label says. Zero types get a dead panel. Guided was verified as a real destination first — rows on all twenty, count equal to the source lines on every one — because replacing one broken promise with a differently worded one was the risk.

Verification then found the ship-blocker in this very patch. Eight lines below it, a block written for the old tab still painted the new one aria-disabled: it read Guided, said the builder does not cover this type, and worked when clicked. Playwright refused the click on aria-disabled grounds — a fair proxy for keyboard and screen-reader users, who were being told a live control was dead.

The app already had the idea; one line named the tab after two of the three builders
Done

The theme collection says what it is offering

Two themes added — Wonders, night and day, from measured contrast rather than taste. The star now marks every theme that moves, KPMG Blue included, and carries a legend and an accessible name; it was a CSS pseudo-element, so a screen reader never mentioned it at all.

And five groups became four. “Signature” held seventeen places with weather; “Worlds” held four static palettes. The names were almost swapped, and what actually separated them — motion — is written on every row already.

39 themes, nothing lost, the quick view still hides nothing below the fold
Parked

Node dragging, after five spikes

Connectors can follow a moved node. The prototype answered the last question and the answer is no: a crude router removes the crossings and destroys the topology, because each edge finds its path independently and two routes through one corridor are drawn on top of each other.

Speed is not the problem — 5.1ms median on an eight-edge hub. What is missing is channel separation, which is where draw.io spends its engineering.

One day of prototype instead of a month of implementation

Shipped today
