Clutter was fought on the wrong axis

Every reduction of the last rounds moved controls further away — twelve toolbar controls became four, the Style card became a shut fold inside a shut fold — while the set of controls itself never shrank by a single item. The same panel is rendered on all twenty diagram types, so 112 of its 260 type-and-control pairs change nothing at all, and 79 of those press back completely normally. Depth-hiding never asks whether a control applies, only whether it fits. So it took away the things used every session and kept every control that does nothing.

Routing is dead on 16, numbering on 15, node spacing on 14. Only flowchart and pie use the whole panel. C4 uses three controls of thirteen and is offered all thirteen.

How far away it all is

Changing the diagram font is three presses to bring it on screen and four to have changed anything — Inspect, then “Style · fonts, colours, legend”, then the Fonts fold, then the control.

Seven controls in the app have the word Style in their label. Re-measured against the viewport rather than the layout: not one of them is visible without scrolling. The nearest, a fold reading “Style · how this diagram looks”, sits 1,401px down. The font control itself is at 1,925px. The button actually labelled ⚙ Style is display: none behind a menu labelled Inspect, so the only signpost is a tooltip — which does not exist on a touch screen.

And a second cost that has nothing to do with distance: set a fill colour in the Style panel, then click the block to look at it, and the colour is gone with no message. Measured — nodeFillColor set to #ff0000, click the block, back to #172230. The two things the app does say at that moment are a sync count and a drag hint. Neither mentions what was just discarded.

Three changes, ranked by impact

01

Make the Style panel type-true

Render only the controls that actually change this diagram type — per control, not per fold. The panel then shows 13 on a flowchart, 5 on a sequence, 3 on C4, and the number of controls on screen becomes a true statement about the diagram in front of you.

Today
A sequence diagram is offered node spacing, rank spacing, connector curve, routing, the palette swatches and class assignment. All inert. All fully enabled.
Removes
Alignment entirely. The palette fold on the 13 types where it is inert. The spacing fold where none of its controls bite. Every hint that exists only to apologise for a control that should not have been rendered.
Note
The lookup table already exists — it is the measured matrix above.
several days
02

The Look bar — and delete the depth it replaces

One permanently visible row above the preview. Each chip shows its current value at rest — “Spacing Comfortable”, not “Spacing” — and expands in place into a tray that pushes the preview down and never covers it. A chip with nothing relevant for the current type is absent, not greyed.

Measured
The prototype is real and rendered: 47px at rest, 193px open, tray bottom exactly at preview top, no overlap, zero console errors.
Why not flat
A flat row of nine controls costs 140px permanently — about 21% of the drawing area — and reflows every time the type changes. The chip bar costs 7%.
Removes
One of the two nested fold levels. The Style row in the Inspect menu. The hidden ⚙ Style button and the CSS rule that hides it. Tour step 6, which is why the tour counts to six and shows four cards. And the duplicate direction control.
a week or more
03

One control per verb

Two block editors carry the same ten fields and two identically labelled “Reset block” buttons, with two different commit models — the inspector commits on change, the panel needs “Apply to block”. That mismatch is what silently eats the fill colour above.

Same disease
Two controls named Guided, of which the working one reported itself unselected until this morning. A command palette harvesting 229 buttons, 181 of which are not on screen.
Removes
The duplicate route, not the capability. Per-block styling belongs to the inspector; diagram-level styling to the bar.
several days

The two questions that needed an answer, not a survey

Hide, disable, or restructure?

Hide. A control that cannot affect the diagram on screen is not rendered.

It is the same rule you stated yourself about the Guided button, applied to the other 111 cases. Disable-with-a-reason loses on arithmetic: it leaves all 79 silent controls on screen, so nothing gets less dense, and it commits you to writing and maintaining 112 true sentences — where the app’s single existing attempt is already false. Greying is right when somebody expects a control to apply and can deduce how to enable it. That describes an unsaved file, not a pie chart that will never accept connector routing.

Two guards. The panel is never empty — title, font and font size work on all twenty types. And the gate is per control, not per fold: the prototype failed this exact test on gitGraph, showing a dead “Between rows” slider inside an otherwise relevant chip. Get that wrong and the lie is rebuilt one level down.

Your expandable button

Yes — but only here, and one question could overturn it.

It works over the small, type-varying style controls because a chip can show its current value at rest, which is the one thing a flat bar cannot do. It does not work for Theme (39 presets), reusable block styles (37 controls) or the legend (23). An expandable holding any of those is the fold you are complaining about wearing a different chevron.

The condition. If your honest answer to “what do you touch most” is three controls rather than nine, put those three flat on the bar with no expansion at all — about 40px, zero clicks, and it beats the chip bar outright. The chip earns its one click only if the set is genuinely nine to thirteen and genuinely varies by type. Answer that before anyone writes the chips.

  • The word “Style” is not visible at rest. Seven candidates, nearest at 1,401px, none in the viewport.
  • The fill colour is silently lost. Driven with a real click.

Not verified by me
