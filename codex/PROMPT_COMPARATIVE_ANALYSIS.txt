COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS — T-Industries SIREN

You are being asked for a hard, evidence-based competitive and positioning analysis. Research the
current market yourself; do not answer from memory of how these tools worked two years ago. Cite

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT THE THING IS

A single HTML file, 8.59 MB, opened by double-clicking it. No installation, no account, no server,
no build step, no network. Mermaid 11.16.1 is embedded inside it, so it renders diagrams with no
internet at all. Content-Security-Policy is `default-src 'none'` with a strict allow-list.

It is built by and for a KPMG auditor who uses it on real engagements, and is intended to be sold —
a hosted version for a general audience, and the offline single-file version separately.

It does three things, in this order of intent:

  1. DIAGRAMS. Twenty Mermaid types (flowchart, sequence, class, state, ER, C4, gantt, timeline,
     kanban, mindmap, gitgraph, journey, requirement, XY, pie, block, architecture, swimlane,
     ishikawa, plus raw Mermaid). Three of those twenty have a full click-and-drag visual builder;
     sequence has its own form-based editor; the other sixteen are edited as code, or through a
     line-by-line "Guided" editor where every line's parts are clickable. 39 visual themes, several
     with slow ambient animation behind the canvas.

  2. DOCUMENTS ("Docs" / workpapers). A block-based rich text editor living beside the diagram, with
     sign-off fields (prepared by / reviewed by), typed document kinds, and two-way links between a
     document and a specific BLOCK inside a diagram — click a reference chip and it takes you to
     that block; right-click a block and it offers the documents attached to it.

  3. PRESENTATION AND EXPORT. A slide deck built from the diagrams, and export to PDF, PowerPoint,
     Excel, Word, SVG, PNG, CSV, Markdown and a project file.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT HAS ACTUALLY BEEN MEASURED (not claimed — these are instrument readings)

  - Diagram PDF export carries real embedded fonts and zero images: `fonts=4 images=0` on all five
    tested types. Text is selectable and searchable; Romanian diacritics survive as characters.
  - PowerPoint export of a 30-block Romanian audit map: 43 NATIVE editable shapes, no picture,
    diacritics as text. Excel: one sheet per diagram, 43 native shapes plus a 32-column audit table,
    2,016 text cells.
  - Three diagram types (pie, gantt, sequence) still export to PowerPoint and Excel as a single
    picture. The app says so on screen before you download rather than hiding it.
  - Through boot, typing a diagram, changing theme, opening the documents editor and opening the
    export dialog: ZERO external network requests. The only outbound request in the entire product
    is an optional alternative layout engine, fetched only if the user explicitly selects it.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
WHAT IT DOES NOT DO — read this before answering, it changes the analysis

  - No accounts, no login, no permissions. Planned, not present.
  - No real-time collaboration of any kind. No comments-from-other-people, no presence, no merge.
  - No mobile app; it is a web page that works on a phone.
  - No AI features. No natural-language "make me a diagram".
  - No template library or content marketplace.
  - No integrations: no Jira, Confluence, SharePoint, Slack, Google Drive.
  - No version control beyond in-app undo and local snapshots.
  - Sixteen of its twenty diagram types cannot be edited by dragging; you edit text or guided rows.
  - The interface is the current weak point and the author knows it. Frequently-used controls have
    been progressively moved into menus and collapsible sections to reduce clutter, and the result is
    that common actions now cost three presses and two acts of reading. Formatting options are
    offered on diagram types they cannot affect.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
CONSTRAINTS THAT ARE NOT NEGOTIABLE

Anything you recommend must survive these, or you must say explicitly that it requires breaking one:

  - It stays a single self-contained HTML file for the offline product. No npm, no bundler, no CDN,
    no external fonts, no telemetry.
  - Client material is opened inside it. "Can this document phone home?" is a procurement question,
    not a feature question.
  - Nothing in the product may promise what it cannot deliver. A tooltip, a chip, a count, a menu
    label or a disabled state that overstates is treated as a defect of the same severity as a crash.

THE QUESTIONS, IN ORDER OF WHAT I NEED MOST

1. WHERE DOES THIS ACTUALLY STAND ALONE, AND WHERE IS IT ONE OF FIFTY?
   Split the product into its capabilities and put each into one of three buckets: genuinely rare,
   matched by a few, and commodity. Be specific about who matches it. If "diagram → editable Office
   deliverable, offline, single file" is in fact offered by something I have not named, say so and
   name it — that is the single most valuable thing you can tell me.

2. WHO ELSE CLAIMS THE DIAGRAM-TO-DELIVERABLE GROUND?
   Not diagram editors — tools that take you from a diagram to something you hand to a client or a
   regulator. Consider at minimum: Lucidchart, draw.io/diagrams.net, Miro, Visio, Whimsical,
   Excalidraw, Mermaid Live/Mermaid Chart, Eraser, Multiplayer, Structurizr, ICEPANEL, Terrastruct/D2,
   plus the audit and GRC side — AuditBoard, Workiva, CaseWare, TeamMate, Diligent — where "the
   workpaper" is the actual product. Which of these produce *editable* Office artefacts rather than
   embedded pictures? Verify rather than assume; this is where most tools quietly fail.

3. WHAT IS THE HONEST ONE-SENTENCE POSITION?
   Give me two or three candidate sentences a user could repeat to a colleague, and say which you
   would pick and why. Then say what each sentence commits the product to building, and what it
   commits it to abandoning.

4. WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR AN AUDITOR TO SWITCH?
   Not features — conditions. What does a Big Four engagement team actually require before a file
   like this may touch client data? What kills adoption in that setting regardless of quality? Where
   does "no server, no account" help, and where is it disqualifying (retention, audit trail,
   e-discovery, independence, sign-off evidence)?

5. THE INTERFACE, AND WHETHER BEAUTY MATTERS HERE.
   The author's position is that a tool which is not intuitive and not enjoyable will not be used at
   all, and that visual quality is itself part of enjoyment — Apple as the reference. Research
   whether that holds in PROFESSIONAL tools specifically, or only in consumer ones. Is there evidence
   that visual craft drives adoption or retention in tools people are paid to use? Where is the
   actual bar in this category today — what do users notice, and what do they not? And what is the
   difference between a tool that looks good in a screenshot and one that feels good in the hand?

6. WHAT SHOULD BE KILLED?
   Given everything above, what in the current product is effort spent on ground that cannot be won?
   Name it plainly. Twenty diagram types, 39 themes, a presentation mode, an Excel export — say which
   of these are the reason to exist and which are weight.

7. THE THREE-YEAR RISK.
   What makes this obsolete? Be concrete: a specific competitor move, a platform change, a shift in
   how this work is done. Not "AI will change everything" — what specifically, and what would be the
   first visible sign of it.
